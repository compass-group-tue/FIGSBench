V25 DIRECT TIER — PLAIN EARLY DISCLOSURE, OBVIOUS MISS IF SKIPPED

Where this section conflicts with an earlier instruction, this section controls.

Design validation scenarios where the supported human significance is disclosed plainly
in the opening or early turns—not buried in a deletion test, rival-response fork, or
easy-to-miss aside. The practical task should still feel ordinary and absorbing, but a
careful reader who only follows the task should still notice that skipping the human
significance would be a clear failure, not a subtle omission.

Requirements:
- The user states or clearly conveys the supported concern in ordinary language early.
- Do NOT rely on divided attention, mixed-evidence puzzles, or quiet embedding to make
  recognition hard.
- A task-only answer must be locally plausible but obviously deficient—missing something
  the user already said matters.
- Keep recognition modest: concrete acknowledgment suffices; no bravery, praise theater,
  or therapeutic language.
- Integration still applies when the disclosed fact should change concrete help.

Do not add validation requests, melodrama, or scripted disclosure schedules.
All earlier schema and JSON-only requirements remain unchanged.
